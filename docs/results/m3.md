## What the Pareto comparison adds

The criterion does not ask the question a reviewer will: does the policy find a
better trade-off, or does it simply curtail more? The cap sweep answers it, and
the answer needed a finer grid than the first attempt — all five policies fell
into a 12.6 MWh gap between the caps at 0.15 and 0.10, where the reference was a
straight line through unmeasured ground.

With caps at 0.10 through 0.15 measured:

- **Four of eight caps are pushed off the Pareto front by policies.** `cap 0.14`
  is dominated by seed `d9ec3b2a`, `cap 0.13` by `eaca5d1d`, and `cap 0.11` and
  `cap 0.10` by both `4c717e86` and `f15fb87c` — each time strictly less
  curtailment *and* less overload.
- **One of five policies is dominated by a cap.** Seed `2738af60` curtails
  46.79 MWh for zero overload, where `cap 0.11` achieves the same with 43.12 MWh.
- At zero overload, the best policy needs **41.21 MWh against the best cap's
  43.12 MWh** — 4.4 % less curtailment, or 2.4 % of the PV energy available in
  those weeks.
- At equal curtailment, the remaining overload differs by **0.07 to 2.48 units**,
  which is 0.02 % to 0.68 % of the uncontrolled value of 365.71.

So the policy is genuinely on the frontier rather than reproducing a cap, but the
margin is small. Both framings agree on that, and both are reported because
neither alone is honest: a percentage against a near-zero reference inflates the
result, and an absolute figure without the uncontrolled scale hides it.

## Three qualifications

**The voltage criterion is not the binding one.** Every cap at 0.40 or tighter
reaches `pass_rate = 1.0`. In this grid and scenario the EN 50160 percentile
criterion is satisfied by almost any curtailment, and the whole budget, potential
and percentile apparatus of section 6.6 contributes little to the comparison.
What binds throughout is thermal overload.

**The reward weighting determines where on the frontier the policy lands.** With
−10 on overload against −1 on curtailment, eliminating overload is worth almost
any yield, and the converged policies curtail 42 % to 58 % of the available PV
energy. That is not a learning defect — it is the specified objective, followed
correctly. A different weighting would place the policy elsewhere on the same
frontier, which is precisely why the Pareto plot rather than a single reward
figure is the reportable result.

**Reference methods B3 and B4 are structurally disadvantaged.** They react to
voltage while the binding constraint is thermal; a local characteristic cannot
see transformer loading. The droop lands within 3 units of overload of `cap 0.40`
at comparable curtailment. Beating it is therefore a weak statement, and M6 needs
a transformer-aware reference — a central limit on aggregate infeed — before the
baseline set can carry a strong claim.

## What the earlier 200,000-step runs were

The first acceptance attempt used three seeds at 200,000 steps and produced an
overload advantage spread between 5 % and 59 %. A diagnostic run over 600,000
steps showed why: the mean episode return is still rising steeply at 200,000, and
its slope only halves every 150,000 steps thereafter. The spread was **budget
noise, not seed variance**. At 600,000 steps the five seeds agree closely —
overload between 0.00 and 1.98, curtailment within a 12 MWh band.

Two defects found along the way are worth recording because both produced
plausible-looking numbers:

- the evaluation sampled episodes with replacement instead of using the fixed
  evaluation set, so each seed was scored on a different selection of weeks and
  the deterministic baselines differed between runs;
- the overload advantage was reported as a percentage of a near-zero reference,
  which turned a difference of two units into "96 % better".

## Consequences for M4

1. **The value of learning has to come from shifting load, not from cutting it.**
   With PV curtailment as the only actuator, the optimal policy is close to a
   fine fixed cap, and the measured edge is a few percent. Storage, heat pumps
   and charge points give the agent something to move rather than only something
   to switch off; that is where a learned controller should separate from a rule.
2. **Consider the Lagrangian reward mode.** In the fixed-weight formulation the
   exchange rate between overload and yield is an arbitrary choice that decides
   the operating point. In the constrained formulation the limits are normative —
   `d = 0.05` is the EN 50160 criterion itself, and the thermal limit is hard —
   and the agent minimises curtailment subject to them. That puts the policy at
   the knee of the frontier rather than at a corner chosen by weights.
3. **A scenario in which the voltage criterion binds.** If EN 50160 is to remain
   a subject of the work rather than a satisfied side condition, M4 needs a
   scenario where it actually constrains. `undervoltage_stress` proved too weak
   and `voltage_stress` is declared an extreme case.

## Correction note (added at the start of M4)

Reading the code against the Lagrangian formulation turned up three things about
how the M3 runs were configured. None of them changes the verdict, which rests on
KPIs measured identically for every controller, but each changes how a number in
this record should be read.

- **The K95 penalty term was identically zero.** In `fixed_weights` mode the
  per-step K95 value was the violating windows summed over buses divided by
  `n_buses × budget_windows`. A 15-minute step closes at most two windows per
  bus, so the value never exceeds 2/50 = 0.04, and the hinge at 0.05 never
  fired. The voltage signal the policies learned from came from the budget
  potential alone. The qualification above -- the voltage criterion does not
  bind -- still holds, because the caps pass without any reward at all; but the
  policies' own handling of the budget was shaped by the potential, not by the
  specified penalty.
- **The overload column is three times the integral its name suggests.** The
  cost sums the excess loading of the three five-minute samples of a control
  step and multiplies by the full step duration of 0.25 h instead of 5 minutes.
  All controllers are affected equally, so rankings, dominance relations and
  percentages are unchanged; read as percent-hours / 100, the absolute values
  divide by three (365.71 becomes 121.90).
- **The PPO settings are the Stable-Baselines3 defaults.** `train.py` does not
  read `configs/agent/ppo.yaml`: the runs used `n_steps` 2,048 per worker,
  `batch_size` 64 and a 64×64 network, not 512, 128 and 256×256 as that file
  states. The configuration hash in the manifests covers the settings actually
  used, so the runs are reproducible; the file is misleading.

The Lagrangian validation keeps all three unchanged so that it is paired with
these runs. Fixing them is a separate change with its own reruns.
